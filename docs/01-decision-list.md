# 01 — Decision List

What a team decides each month, as the engine runs it today, and the original
56-field design it grew from.

- **As built** (below) is the authority: the table is generated from the
  engine's registry (`src/ecomsim/decisions.py`) by `tools/doc_tables.py`,
  and `tests/test_docs.py` fails if the two ever disagree.
- **The original design** (at the end) is kept for reference, with a map of
  which fields were built, built differently, or left out.

The founding round (month 0) has its own decision set: see `05-start-mode.md`.

---

## How a decision reaches the engine

1. **Preset and month.** A decision is the team's to set when it is enabled in
   the game's preset and its opening month has come; otherwise the engine runs
   its default (M0, `Resolver`). The live course runs the *advanced* preset.
2. **The instructor's windows win.** On the admin *Decisions* page a lever can
   be opened early or held back month by month. A closed lever runs on its
   default even if a team saved a value before it closed (2026-10-13).
   `15-course-map.md` gives the recommended session-by-session opening.
3. **Per-game switches.** Some levers exist only in games set up on the rules
   that brought them (column *Offered*). A game already trading when a rule
   arrived keeps its old behaviour until the instructor switches it on from a
   month not yet run (see `04-configurability.md`, *Per-game rules*).
4. **A lever left alone keeps last month's setting** (rules v2, from month 3 of
   the Fall-2026 game, month 1 of any new game). The price grid carries line by
   line; ticking *Keep* records last month's setting as this month's decision.
   One-off decisions are never carried: market research (12.1), the board memo
   (12.5), a typed stock order (7.1), range changes (1.3), returns to the
   supplier (7.6), AI builds (11.x), the loan (12.6) and extra repayment (12.7).
5. **Saving.** The form saves every change as it is made (autosave) and the
   Submit button saves the whole form; a team may revise until the month is
   closed or run. A month whose stock, marketing, other budgets, research and AI builds
   come to more than cash plus the approved loan cannot be submitted: the
   change is kept as a draft, the form says why, and the admin page flags
   *changes not submitted*. A blank stock order never blocks a save - it buys
   what the month can pay for.
6. **In administration** (cash below zero with the credit line full),
   marketing budgets (3.1-3.9), website (5.1), CRM (6.1) and quality assurance (7.4) are
   capped at half of the month before; AI builds (11.x) and research (12.1)
   are blocked.

### Notes on the levers that need more than a line

- **1.1 Range, prices and sourcing.** One row per product in the range: price,
  discount and local or imported sourcing. Cost follows sourcing; the product
  mix each team sells follows its customers, prices, campaigns, packs and
  stock (2026-10-08). In like-for-like games the price index compares each
  team's shelf with the same products at catalogue price (2026-10-15).
- **1.2 Bundles.** Up to three three-packs count. Uptake follows the saving
  against three singles: a few convenience buyers at no saving, nothing at all
  above three singles, more with each point of saving up to a ceiling. The
  saving is given away on every pack unit, so a small saving (about 5%) earns
  most and past about 12% a pack loses money (2026-10-08).
- **1.3 Range changes.** From month 3, product by product: an added product is
  ordered at once (a month of expected sales, normal supplier terms) and goes
  on sale when it lands; a dropped product stops selling at once and its stock
  goes back refunded less a 20% restocking fee. The range stays 12-18 products.
- **2.4 Free-delivery threshold.** A trade-off, not a free lift: free delivery
  on everything lifts conversion 4% and shrinks baskets 3%; a bar up to half a
  basket above a typical order lifts basket size; beyond that small buyers walk
  away; items added to reach the bar carry their cost of goods (2026-10-07).
- **3.10 Campaign setup.** Splits each Meta, TikTok and Google budget into up to
  three campaigns aimed by product, age, gender, city tier, interests, language
  and format. The report shows CTR, CPC, CVR, ROAS and CAC per campaign, and
  each channel's buying intent shares the paid orders out (Google Search 1.8,
  Meta 1.0, TikTok 0.6).
- **3.6 Affiliate & influencer commission.** In games on working affiliates
  (new games, and the Fall-2026 game from the month switched on) partners bring
  visitors - more the higher the rate, with the same diminishing returns as
  paid media; 30% would have come anyway and are taken from organic; their
  customers churn about 20% a month; commission is paid only on the sales they
  track (2026-10-17).
- **4.1 Marketplace.** Listing puts the team in front of a pool of marketplace
  shoppers shared by rank (half price, half rating) with the other listed teams
  and the marketplace's own sellers; 10% of own-site orders move across and pay
  commission; payout 15 days after the sale; marketplace customers buy less
  often and leave faster (2026-10-13).
- **5.1 Website experience.** Each month's spend closes part of the gap to the
  tech stack's UX ceiling - half the gap at PKR 300,000 - and the gain lands the
  following month (2026-10-03).
- **7.1 Stock to purchase.** A total (split by what sold), or product by
  product; a split below the supplier's minimum is scaled up in proportion.
  Left blank, the engine orders up to cycle + lead time + safety cover from its
  demand forecast, as far as cash and the approved loan go; the form shows the
  units and cost it will buy, or that it falls below the supplier's minimum and
  nothing will be ordered (2026-10-22). Stock always lands the month after it is
  ordered at the earliest.
- **7.6 Return stock to supplier.** Units product by product, never more than
  the team holds; the supplier keeps 50% of landed cost as a restocking fee and
  refunds the rest in cash that month (2026-10-18). Stock held costs 2.1% of its
  value a month, and cover beyond six months is written down 10% a month.
- **11.x Technology and AI.** Projects with a build cost paid at the start, a
  build time, a running cost once live and a published chance of working,
  rolled when the build completes. A failed build is not refunded; the report,
  the form and the console show every build as building, live or failed
  (2026-10-22).

  | # | Module | Build cost | Build time | Running cost a month | Chance it works | Effect when live |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 11.1 | Recommendation engine | PKR 3,000,000 | 2 months | PKR 250,000 | 85% | Average order value about 7% higher |
  | 11.2 | AI demand forecasting | PKR 2,200,000 | 2 months | PKR 180,000 | 80% | Forecast error 40% smaller (lead time unchanged) |
  | 11.3 | Dynamic pricing | - | - | - | - | **Withdrawn**: never read by the engine; every build and running cost refunded once, in cash (2026-10-22) |
  | 11.4 | AI customer service | PKR 1,800,000 | 1 month | PKR 120,000 | 90% | Each agent answers 2.5 times as many contacts |
  | 11.5 | AI creative generation | PKR 1,200,000 | 1 month | PKR 90,000 | 75% | Creative floor raised, ceiling capped |

- **12.5 Board memo.** Read by the instructor, ignored by the model. Under rules
  v2 it carries 2 of the 10 decision-quality points unless the instructor marks
  it as not matching the month's decisions (from the console or the preview).
- **12.6 Working capital loan / 12.7 Extra repayment.** The form shows what the
  team owes and what the month needs - supplier bills due, the stock it is
  ordering, marketing and fixed costs, less cash and money arriving - with a
  one-click Approve for the suggested amount. Up to the PKR 24M limit, at 22% a
  year on what is drawn. If the month still cannot be funded the engine cuts
  the stock order first, then marketing and other spending; bills already owed
  are paid regardless (the bank covers the gap, reported as unplanned
  borrowing). Cash above a month of fixed costs repays the loan automatically;
  12.7 repays more (2026-10-16).

### Decision-quality rubric (for 12.5, as designed)

Graded 0–10 per round on four criteria, not on outcome:

1. **Evidence** — does the memo cite the team's own data or purchased research?
2. **Causal reasoning** — does it name the mechanism, not just the metric?
3. **Trade-off acknowledgement** — does it state what was sacrificed?
4. **Falsifiability** — does it state what result would prove the decision wrong?

Criterion 4 is the one that matters. Teams that write "we expect revenue up"
score low; teams that write "if repeat rate does not exceed 26% by Round 7, this
retention investment was wrong" score high.
