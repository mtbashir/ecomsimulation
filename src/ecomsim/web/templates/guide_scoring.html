2 · How a measure is marked

Most measures have three published anchors: the value that scores nothing, the value that scores half, and the value that scores full marks. In between, marks rise in a straight line; beyond the ends they stop. A measure worth 8 points at its half-way anchor earns 4.

Example. Market share gained is worth 7 points: losing 2 points of share scores nothing, holding level half, gaining 2.5 points full. Gaining 1.25 points is half-way from level to full, so it earns 5.25.

Profitability: against the best team

{% if rules_from > 1 %}From month {{ rules_from }}, each{% else %}Each{% endif %} margin is marked against the best in the room: the best team earns full marks, every other team the same share as its margin is of the best's. A margin of 10% against a best of 40% earns a quarter of the points. A negative margin earns nothing.

Runway: a band, not the most

Runway is your cash over a month's cash burn at the end. Under 1.5 months scores nothing, 1.5 to 3 months 30%, 3 to 9 months full marks, 9 to 16 months 60%, more than 16 months 30%. A business that is not burning cash at all counts as more than 16 months. Running out of cash scores nothing on the whole cash pillar.

3 · Which months count

Most measures are taken across the whole run, and later months count more: each month's weight is its number out of 78, so month 12 carries about 15% and month 1 about 1%. A weak start can be recovered; a strong start thrown away cannot.

Margins are the margins of the whole period — total profit over total revenue — not an average of monthly percentages, so a fat margin on a tiny month cannot lift the score. Revenue growth compares your last month with your first. Active customers, NPS, runway and cash are read as they stand at the end.

5 · Where you see it

Your monthly report

From month 2, the report ends with Scorecard, as it stands: your points on each pillar against its weight. Only your own score is shown.

Rankings

Your instructor publishes the rankings, usually from month 4, so every team settles on a strategy before comparing itself with rivals. Once they are out, the Rankings page shows every month up to the latest published: the cumulative score as it stood that month, and the month on its own - the same scorecard applied to that month alone, with growth against the month before. The month on its own shows who had the best month; only the cumulative score becomes your final score, published after month 12.

6 · What the scorecard punishes

Buying growth

Deep discounts and heavy ads raise revenue and orders, then cost you on margins, customer value and cash. Growth is 20 points; the pillars it can damage are 70.

Harvesting at the end

Cutting marketing in the last months lifts the margin for a moment, but the final months carry the most weight, revenue falls against your first month, and the customer base shrinks.

Hoarding cash

Runway is a band, not “most wins”: more than 9 months of idle cash scores less than 3 to 9 months. Running out scores nothing on the whole cash pillar.

Coasting

Leaving the levers alone keeps last month's settings, but a month in which nothing was weighed shows no strategy and no thinking, and decision quality is marked that way. Look at every lever, act or keep it on purpose, and say in your board memo what you decided and why.
